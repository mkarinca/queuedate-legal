Common questions

Where does the delivery date come from?
QueueDate counts the orders currently waiting to be produced, divides that number by your daily production capacity, and adds the result to today's date as working days. Transit days are then added to get the delivery date. Closure days and non-working days are skipped.
Which orders count as being in the queue?
Open, unfulfilled orders. Once an order is fulfilled or cancelled it leaves the queue and the date moves earlier for everyone still waiting.
The date on my product page is not updating.
Check three things. First, that the delivery date block is still present in your theme — a theme change can remove it. Second, that the app proxy path in the block settings matches /apps/eta. Third, whether your storefront is serving a cached page; hard refresh the product page to confirm.
Nothing appears where the block should be.
The block hides itself when it has no date to show, rather than displaying an empty space. This happens when the queue is too long to estimate. Set a long lead time message in the block settings to display text in that case.
What does the cutoff time do?
Orders placed after the cutoff start production the next working day. Set it to the time you stop taking work into the workshop each day.
Can production and shipping use different calendars?
Yes. Working days control when you produce, and carrier delivery days control when the courier moves parcels. A workshop closed at weekends can still have Saturday deliveries.
How do I change or cancel my plan?
Uninstalling the app from your Shopify admin ends the subscription. Charges appear on your regular Shopify invoice.
What data does the app access?
Order records, used only to count how many orders are waiting. The app does not read customer details. See the privacy policy for the full description.
