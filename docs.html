Setting up QueueDate

From install to a live delivery date on your product pages.

1. Set your production capacity

Open the app and go to Settings. These four values decide every date the app shows:

Daily production capacity How many orders you finish on an average working day.
Transit days How long the courier takes once a parcel leaves you.
Cutoff time Orders placed after this time start production the next working day.
Working days The days your workshop produces. Carrier delivery days are set separately, so a closed Saturday can still be a delivery day.

Save, then return to the dashboard. It shows how many orders are waiting and the date those settings produce, so you can check the result before customers see it.

2. Add the block to your theme

On the dashboard, click Add to theme. This opens your theme editor with the QueueDate block already selected and placed on a product page.

  1. Drag the block to where you want the message to appear. Under the price or below the add-to-cart button both work well.
  2. Click Save in the theme editor.
  3. Open any product page on your storefront to confirm the message appears.

If you change themes later, add the block again in the new theme. Blocks do not carry across themes.

3. Write the message

The block settings hold the text customers read. Four variables are available:

  • {queue} — orders currently in the production queue
  • {ship_date} — estimated ship date
  • {delivery_date} — estimated delivery date
  • {capacity} — orders produced per day

A message such as Made to order — currently {queue} orders in queue. Ships by {ship_date}, delivered around {delivery_date}. tells the customer both the date and the reason behind it.

Set the long lead time message as well. It replaces the normal text when the queue grows past the point where a date can be estimated, so the block never goes silent during a busy period.

4. Add your closure days

In Settings, add annual leave, public holidays, and any other days the workshop is closed. Closed days are skipped when counting production days, so a week off in August pushes every date forward automatically instead of promising work that will not happen.

Keeping dates accurate

The queue updates as orders arrive and as you fulfil them, so dates move on their own. Two things are worth revisiting: your daily capacity, if you take on help or lose it, and your closure calendar at the start of each year.
